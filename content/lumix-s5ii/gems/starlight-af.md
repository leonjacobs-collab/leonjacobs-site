When you're shooting in extremely low light, the camera first activates **Low Illumination AF** (a `LOW` icon appears in the viewfinder). If the camera then detects stars in the night sky, it automatically engages **Starlight AF** (a `STAR` icon appears) and locks focus on individual stars [^manual].

The AF area shows you the exact star it focused on. Ewen Bell uses this regularly for aurora and Milky Way work and confirms it works reliably when the conditions are right [^bell].

**Important limitation:** Starlight AF doesn't work at the edges of the frame. Aim at stars in the central portion of the image, then recompose if needed.

If Starlight AF won't engage (thin cloud, bright moon, light pollution), fall back to manual focus with Focus Peaking. Bell's tip: when peaking colour shifts so stars "twinkle blue instead of white," that colour shift is your confirmation of critical focus [^bell].
