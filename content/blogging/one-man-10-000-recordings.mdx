I love these kinds of stories.

One Chicago music nut, a Aadam Jacobs (no relation, I think), has recorded a lifetime of music concerts. [Over 10'000 in total.](https://blockclubchicago.org/2026/04/10/from-early-nirvana-to-phish-a-chicago-fans-secret-recordings-of-10000-shows-are-now-online/)

These stretch back so far in time, that some of the recordings contain early concerts from the likes of Nirvana and Dave Grohl. And apparently the progression of recording fidelity over time is apparent. I guess, a bit like scanning your eye across the Sistine Chapel's ceiling and noticing how Michelangelo's painting style ~~improved~~ evolved over the matters of meters, resembling years of painting, flat on his back.

Apparently, some of Jacobs' enthusiastic buddies are committing the recordings to digital memory, onto the Internet Archive - which apparrently, worryingly, [is currently under threat](https://www.wired.com/story/the-internets-most-powerful-archiving-tool-is-in-mortal-peril/).

(I really hope it survives. It is one of those magical artifacts of the Web, that seems a bit impossible but true. Like Wikipedia and Youtube. And projects like this music archive proves the need for its existence.)
